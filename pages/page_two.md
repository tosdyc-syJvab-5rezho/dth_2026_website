# Open Source Software 

## Disadvantages of an open source software

Despite its many benefits, Open source software also carries some disadvantages. One common misconception is that because OSS is free to download and use, it is also free to maintain and develop. However, installation, updates, maintenance, and technical support may involve additional costs. For example, a municipality choosing open source software might save money on license but still need to pay IT specialist to install, manage, and maintain the system. There might also be additional costs for employee training or transferring data from an old system.

Secondly, some programs can be difficult to install or use, especially for people without technological knowledge. For example, a student using GIMP, an open source image editing program, might find it difficult to learn how to use all its tools and functions.

Moreover, the lack of professional support, meaning that users may have to rely on online communities or forums to find solutions to their problems. 

Finally, many open source projects rely heavily on unpaid volunteers for their development and maintenance. As Madge and Bennett (2024) explain, this can create a sustainability crisis, especially when contributors experience burnout, change careers or lose interest in the project. As a result, the software may become outdated or even abandoned. This can be a problem for users, such as small municipalities or businesses, that depend on these tools for their daily activities. It can also create security risks, as bugs and vulnerabilities may no longer be fixed.
